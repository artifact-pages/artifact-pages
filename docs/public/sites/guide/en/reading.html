For readers

Reading

Published documents open from an ordinary URL. This page explains the screen and how to find pages and sites while you read.

URLs

A URL is the site ID followed by the file's location in the source. Filenames and extensions stay in the URL, so a shared link or a reload opens the same document.

URLOpensExample
/The list of sites/
/<site>The site's home page/checkout
/<site>/<path>A document/checkout/design/payment-flow.html

The example site and path are illustrative. index.html is opened by name like any other document; a folder URL does not open it automatically.

The screen

Site navigation is on the left, the document is in the middle, and a panel opens on the right when you need it.

  1. Site: the site you are reading. Select it to choose another site.
  2. Pinned: documents you pinned, saved only in your browser.
  3. Browse: documents in their folder structure. Narrow it with the field above.
  4. Document: HTML appears exactly as published; Markdown is rendered by the app.
  5. Contents / Details: headings, plus last-update and source information, in a panel over the document.

Sites with many documents also show Recently updated in the sidebar and on the home page.

Search and site switching

Open the command palette with ⌘K / Ctrl K. Plain text searches only the site you are reading, by title and path. A leading symbol changes what you search.

TypeSearches
(no symbol)Documents in the current site. With a document open, its headings and commands are also suggested.
@Sites. Choosing one opens that site's home page.
#Headings in the open document.
>Commands, such as switching the theme or copying a link.

When searching documents, the tabs at the top narrow the scope: All, Recent (documents you read recently), Pinned, and Previews (pull-request previews).

There is no search across several sites at once. To find a document in another site, move to that site with @ first.

HTML and Markdown

HTML is shown exactly as published. Its own CSS and JavaScript run as written, so reports, dashboards, and diagrams look the way their authors built them.

Markdown is rendered by the app for easy reading. It supports GitHub-style tables, task lists, and footnotes, plus Mermaid diagrams. Embedded HTML is partly removed for safety, and scripts do not run.

In both, links to other documents in the same site move within the app.

Contents and details

Contents and Details above the document open a panel on the right without changing the document's width.

  • Contents: the document's headings. Select one to jump to it.
  • Details: the last-updated date, who committed that change, and links to the source repository and file. The committer is recorded for the latest change and is not necessarily the original author.

What stays in your browser

Pins, recently read documents (up to 20 per site), and the theme (light, dark, or follow the system) are saved only in the browser you are using. They are not shared with other people or devices, and they do not change published documents or repositories.

To change a document's content, change it in its source Git repository.
